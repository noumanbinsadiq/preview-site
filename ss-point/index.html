01 — The situation

Why nothing moved

The inventory landed in May. The TikTok Shop registration was rejected because the business address on file traced back to an agent rather than to you, and by the time that could be corrected the momentum was gone.

I want to be straight about the diagnosis, because it changes everything that follows. The address was the trigger, not the cause. The real problem was that the plan required building an audience from zero — a storefront, content, creators, an advertising budget — before a single unit could sell. That is a long road, and it stops completely the moment any one piece of it fails. It did.

The decision

Rather than repair that route, this proposal abandons it. No TikTok Shop, no storefront of any kind. Nothing in this plan depends on a platform approving anything.

02 — The approach

Fifteen buyers, not three thousand

Sensory fidget toys and inexpensive giftable self-care items are bought in bulk every single day — by therapy clinics, school districts, party-prize wholesalers, subscription-box companies, and by online sellers who already have traffic. These buyers take 100 to 3,000 units at a time, on a purchase order or a simple invoice.

Reaching them costs nothing but email. The professional product photography is already paid for on your original invoice, so there is no creative spend either. And when it is finished, it is genuinely finished: five to fifteen bulk shipments out of the garage instead of three thousand individual orders, each with its own packing, postage and customer.

04 — The buyers

Six channels, worked in order

Ranked by the price each can pay, not by how easy they are. The money is at the top of this list; the certainty is at the bottom.

01 · Therapy & school institutions

Fidget and sensory tools are a standing budget line for special education, pediatric occupational therapy and ABA autism programs. They buy in bulk, on purchase orders, from small vendors routinely — and August to October is exactly when they spend. Best pricing of any channel.

02 · Party, carnival & prize wholesalers

These companies buy closeout novelty stock as a matter of course and resell it as prizes. Halloween and school-carnival buying peaks in September, which puts the slime and fidget stock right in the middle of their season.

03 · Subscription box companies

A box needs several hundred identical, giftable, low-cost units every month and is permanently hunting for them. Sourcing happens by direct email to the founder — no gatekeeper. 600 units is exactly one month's run for a mid-size box.

04 · Sellers who already have traffic

Active TikTok Shop, Amazon and Walmart sellers pay real money for stock already sitting in the US, because it ships immediately instead of waiting six weeks on an import. Rather than competing with them for attention, we sell to them.

05 · Faire wholesale marketplace

Free to list, and it puts all five products in front of hundreds of thousands of boutiques and gift shops that order at wholesale. One afternoon of setup, then it runs quietly in the background alongside everything else.

06 · Closeout liquidators — the safety net

Anything still unsold after four weeks goes to closeout buyers or Dallas pallet wholesalers as one lot. This roughly returns your cost and guarantees the garage ends up empty. It exists so there is no version of this where you are still holding stock in November.

05 — Execution

How it actually runs

The outreach runs on an automated operator working from a written brief. It researches buyers, writes and personalises every email, sends from your inbox within legal sending limits, answers replies, builds a personal quote page for each serious buyer with your PayPal and Zelle details on it, and reports every week. It works each weekday without being asked.

What I handle

Finding and qualifying every buyer · writing and sending all outreach · answering enquiries and negotiating down to the agreed floors · producing quotes and payment links · reporting weekly.

What you handle

Confirming payments have landed in your account · packing and shipping five to fifteen bulk orders · approving any price below the agreed floor. Roughly an hour a day, and less once orders consolidate.

On the money
