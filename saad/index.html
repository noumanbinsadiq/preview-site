01The arrangement

What you are actually buying

You want to own a product brand on Amazon. The hard part is not running it — your team can run it. The hard part is the first ninety days: choosing a product that can actually win, finding a factory that will hold a specification, building a brand that looks like it belongs at its price, and writing a listing that converts. Get those four wrong and no amount of good operating fixes it.

That is what I do, once, for a fixed fee. I choose the product, prove the demand, vet and negotiate the supplier, build the entire brand, write the listing, plan the launch, and hand you the operating manual. Then it is yours — the company, the trademark, the supplier relationship, the Amazon account, the customer data, all of it in your name from day one. I take no equity and no ongoing cut.

Product selection with the competitive evidence behind it · supplier shortlist, sample coordination and negotiation · brand name, logo and full identity · packaging design, print-ready · photography art direction and shot list · complete Amazon listing and A+ content · 90-day launch plan with advertising structure · your own copy of the financial model · a full setup checklist · one recorded handover session.

05The finding that surprised me

Capital is the ceiling, not demand

When I ran the model at different starting amounts, the result was not what I expected. The business does not fail because nobody wants the product. It fails because it runs out of stock while demand is still rising.

Manufacturing takes about thirty days. Ocean freight takes another thirty to thirty-five. So a reorder placed today arrives in two months. If the launch works, demand outruns the first production run before the second one lands — and the listing goes out of stock. A stockout is not a pause. It resets your organic ranking, kills your review velocity, and hands your position to a competitor. Recovering costs more than the sales you lost.

What this means for you

The extra $5,000 between a $10,000 start and a $15,000 start is worth roughly $45,000 of first-year revenue. It is the highest-return money in the entire plan. If you would rather stage your risk, the alternative is to start at $12,500 and commit — in advance — to a $5,000 top-up in month six. That recovers most of the gap. What does not work is starting small and hoping.

The corollary is uncomfortable and I want it said plainly: every dollar of profit is reinvested for the first eighteen months. No drawings. If you need income from this business in year one, this plan is the wrong plan.

06The asset

What the business could be worth

Amazon brands are bought and sold. There is a real market with published transaction data, and I have used realised sale prices rather than the multiples brokers advertise — those are two very different numbers.

The honest multiple 2.0×

Brokers market "3–5× annual profit." Across 161 completed sales in 2025 at the largest marketplace for these businesses, the realised blended average was about 2.0× annual profit. Under $300,000 — which is 78% of all deals — it was 1.87×.

The single biggest lever +108%

E-commerce businesses with a registered trademark sold for an average of $269,987 against $122,852 without — a 108.5% price premium. This is why the trademark is filed in month one, not month twelve.

The discount to avoid −50%

Businesses classed as distressed — declining, single-product, owner-dependent, or with account problems — cleared at roughly half the typical multiple. Most of the work in months 12–24 exists to stay out of that band.

Company → Tax ID (2–6 weeks) → Payment account → Amazon → Trademark → Barcodes → Supplier & customs → Insurance

08The part most proposals leave out

What can go wrong, honestly

I told you I would give you a plan I believe in. I do. I also said that profit and loss are both part of business, and that is the part this section is about. Here is what the actual data says about people who do what you are about to do.

The base rates

New sellers launching on Amazon in 2025 fell 44% to 165,000 — the lowest since tracking began. The number of active sellers dropped from 2.4 million in 2021 to 1.65 million at the end of 2025. Of sellers still trading, 23% are growing revenue and margin together; 38% are classed as distressed. Around six in ten who survive their first year report reaching profitability — but only about one in five of those ever exceeds $10,000 a month in sales, and that level is still below the minimum a broker will list a business at.

Adjusting for everyone who quit and therefore never answered a survey, my honest estimate is that fewer than one in ten people who launch a private label brand reach a size worth selling. This plan is built to put you in that group. It cannot guarantee you land there.

Three points where we stop rather than push on

Gate 1 · Before ordering

If samples from five factories cannot verifiably hold the specification the whole positioning depends on, we do not order. We change product. This is the most important gate in the plan.

Gate 2 · Month 3

Reviews under 25, conversion under 8%, or advertising costs not trending down — we diagnose before spending more, rather than after.

Gate 3 · Month 6

Advertising above 30% of sales, negative margin, or returns above 6% — we sell down the remaining stock rather than fund a structural loss. Losing $6,000 beats losing $15,000.

What I am not promising

I am not promising profitability. I am not promising a sale, or a multiple. I am not promising that tariffs, shipping rates, or Amazon's own rules hold — all three moved sharply in 2026 and will move again. I am not promising nobody copies you.

What I am promising is this: every deliverable in section 01, built to specification, on the agreed timeline, with every number sourced and every assumption written down so you can challenge it. If a number changes, you hear it from me before you spend, not after.

09The process

How this actually runs

Six phases from the day we start to the day you are trading. Weeks one to eight run in parallel — the company paperwork and the product work happen at the same time, because the tax ID alone can take six weeks.

Weeks 1–2 · Foundation
Company, tax ID, payment account, factory shortlist
Eight to ten factories approached with a written specification. The ones that dodge the specification question are eliminated regardless of price.
Weeks 3–5 · Proof
Samples from five factories, measured and tested
Trademark filed. Amazon account verified. Brand identity drafted. This is where we find out if the plan survives contact with reality.
Weeks 6–8 · Commitment
Supplier chosen, 30% deposit, production starts
Written tariff classification obtained. Photography booked. Packaging designed. Listing and enhanced content written. Terms are always 30% deposit, 70% only against a passed inspection.
Weeks 9–12 · Manufacture
Production, independent inspection, shipping
Progress photos at day 7 and day 14. Independent pre-shipment inspection before the balance is released. If it fails, we do not pay and we do not ship.
Weeks 13–17 · Arrival
Ocean freight, customs, prep, into Amazon
During transit everything else is finished: listing built, photography retouched, advertising campaigns structured and paused, ready to switch on.
Week 18 · Launch — handover complete
Live, advertising running, plan handed to your team
One recorded handover session. From here your team operates it against the 90-day plan. My work is done and my fee is paid in full.
