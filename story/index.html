Every deadline, every follow-up, every balance: in one person's head

Quote requests arrived by text and email, were screenshotted and dropped into a group chat for the team. Four or five more chats carried follow-ups, balances, estimates and receipts, with one person answering most of them. Receipts were typed by hand into a profit sheet whose numbers had quietly stopped working. Follow-ups ran on a reminder plugin. And accounting software had been bought the week before, with no time to learn it.

The owner's own word for it was "a disaster". It wasn't a failing company, though. It was a company doing well and growing fast, and the owner still answered every client within half an hour, personally. That was the one thing no system was allowed to take away.

Chapter 4 · What Nouman realized

Contractors aren't short of programs.

There is plenty of it, and most of it asks the company to change how it works to fit the program. Only about a third of GCs use an estimating program at all (Houzz, 2025).

What was missing was simpler and harder: the owner's rules, written down where the team can carry them out.

Held in one head
  • What's our markup on this one?
  • Can this quote go out, or does it need my yes?
  • When do we chase it?
  • Is the extra work signed before we start?
  • Who's allowed to see the money?
→
Written once, carried out
  • Quotes priced on the owner's markup
  • Nothing goes out without the owner's yes
  • Followed up on the days the owner picks
  • Extra work priced and signed before the crew starts
  • Receipts and photos land on the right job
  • The team sees the work, never the money

Your company, run on your rules. You make the calls; the system carries them out.

Chapter 5 · What building it taught us

Six lessons. Six rules in every system.

It was not a smooth build. Contractors are busy, requirements move, and money is never abstract. Every hard lesson became a rule that now ships with every system.

Use the company's own words

"Margin" was correct English and wrong for them. Their screen says "Profit %", because that's what they call it.

Now: your words on your screens, and your crew's language

Money gets a worked example first

A pricing formula agreed in writing was built exactly as written, and on a real quote it priced the job far too high. Their own estimator caught it, not us.

Now: nothing that touches money goes on until it matches your own paperwork, to the cent

Check for the data, not the file

A backup can pass every check and still be empty.

Now: every backup is proved by restoring it and finding your own records, with a nightly copy in your own Google Drive

Every promise lives in writing

When a term was once restated from memory, it was wrong.

Now: every price, stage and change is written first, and the record wins over anyone's memory, ours included

A moving target protects nobody

When requirements kept moving, both sides lost.

Now: a one-page agreement, four fixed stages, and changes priced and signed before work starts

The team sees the work, never the money

The crew needs tomorrow's job, not the owner's numbers.

Now: every person sees exactly their part, and the owner sees what every job left
